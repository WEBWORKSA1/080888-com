The million-dollar digits

For most people a phone number is a utility. In Chinese-speaking markets, the right digits are an asset with a public auction price.

Landmark sales

  • 2003 · Chengdu. Sichuan Airlines paid about US$280,000 for +86 28 8888 8888. The phone line became part of the brand.
  • 2008 · Beijing Olympics. Before 08-08-08, custom numbers ending 6888 or 1888 sold for over US$3,000. Basic handsets cost as little as US$10.
  • 2017. A number ending in eight 7s reportedly sold for ¥3.91 million (≈US$563,000). Long runs of one digit command a premium even when the digit isn't 8.
  • 2020 · Beijing court auction. A number ending 88888 sold for ¥2.25 million (≈US$324,000) after more than 5,000 bids.
  • Feb 2025 · Hong Kong. At the Transport Department's Lunar New Year auction, plate “88” sold for HK$11.4 million; the single letter “S” went for HK$14.2 million.

What drives the premium

  1. Meaning: 8 (prosper), 6 (smooth), 9 (lasting) raise value; any 4 lowers it.
  2. Scarcity: short plates and long runs (AAAAA) are rare.
  3. Visibility: business lines and car plates are seen by clients, so they work as status signals.
  4. Timing: demand peaks before Lunar New Year and before auspicious dates.

What this means for you

You don't need a seven-figure plate to benefit. Changing a business line from “…0414” to “…0168” costs little and removes a negative signal. Score your numbers or request a better one.

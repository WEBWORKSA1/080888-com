Lucky numbers for business: the playbook

If your customers include Chinese speakers, and in Vancouver, Toronto, Sydney, Singapore, the Bay Area or London they very likely do, your numbers are part of your brand. Here's how to use them.

1. Price endings

End prices in 8 or 88 (e.g. $38, $88, $168, $1,688, $8,888), and use 6 and 9 as secondary endings. Never end a price in 4 and never use 250. Chinese e-commerce builds whole events on numbers: JD.com's “618” festival (June 18) and the 5/20 “I love you” gifting day.

2. Phone numbers

A business line ending in 8, 88, 168 or 888 signals success and is easier to remember. A line with 4s, especially 14 or 514, works against you. Score your line, then request a better one.

3. Addresses, units and floors

Many developers skip floors 4, 13, 14 and 44, or rename them. Units ending in 8 can sell for more in markets with many Chinese buyers. Realtors should check the address score before choosing listing photos and copy.

4. Launch and opening dates

Open on the 8th, 18th or 28th, in August, or on a lunar day ending in 8. Avoid the 7th lunar month. Use the Auspicious Date Finder to get a shortlist.

5. Red envelopes, gifts and bonuses

Give even amounts. 88, 168, 188, 888 and 1,688 are classic. Avoid anything with 4. For weddings, 520 and 1314 are romantic options. Don't give gifts in sets of four.

6. Domains, SKUs and brand names

Numeric domains are common in China because digits are easier to type than pinyin (think 163.com, 360.cn, 58.com). A brand name containing 8, 88 or 168 reads as “prosperous” before anyone has seen the product.

Checklist

  • ☐ No 4, 14, 74, 250 in prices, SKUs, phone or unit numbers
  • ☐ Key prices end in 8 or 88
  • ☐ Business line scores 62+ on the analyzer
  • ☐ Opening date avoids the 7th lunar month
  • ☐ Lunar New Year promotion planned (see guide)

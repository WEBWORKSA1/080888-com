Why numbers are money

The lucky-number economy, in numbers

In Mandarin and Cantonese, digits are heard as words. 8 sounds like 发 (fā, “to prosper”), so it earns a premium. 4 sounds like 死 (sǐ, “death”), so it costs you. The effect shows up in prices:

  • Phone numbers. Sichuan Airlines paid about US$280,000 for +86 28 8888 8888 in 2003. A Beijing court auction sold a number ending in five 8s for ¥2.25 million (≈US$324,000) in 2020.
  • Licence plates. Hong Kong's Lunar New Year auction in February 2025 sold the plate “88” for HK$11.4 million.
  • Dates. About 9,000 Beijing couples booked weddings for 08-08-08, more than double the previous single-day record. The Olympics opened that day at 8:08:08 pm.
  • Buildings and retail. Many towers skip the 4th, 14th and 44th floors. Prices ending in 8 or 88 are standard in Chinese retail.

Questions people ask

Why is 8 so lucky in Chinese culture?
8 (bā) sounds like 发 (fā), “to prosper / get rich”. Its shape, with two closed loops, is also read as endless flow. Beijing picked 8:08:08 pm on 08/08/2008 to open the Olympics for that reason.
What does 080888 mean?
Read líng bā líng bā bā bā, it opens with 0 (wholeness, a fresh start), repeats the 08 prosperity pair, and ends with 888 (triple prosperity). A cycle of wealth that keeps multiplying. Full story.
Is the analyzer scientific?
It is a cultural model of how Chinese-speaking buyers perceive digits, built on homophones and documented market behaviour. It doesn't predict luck. It does predict how a number will be received, and that affects prices, resale value and customer response.

Why buildings skip the 4th floor

Take an elevator in Hong Kong, Shanghai, Singapore or Richmond, BC, and the buttons may jump from 3 to 5, from 13 to 15, or from 39 to 50.

Tetraphobia, briefly

Fear of the number 4 (tetraphobia) comes from the sound of 四 (sì), close to 死 (sǐ, death). It's shared across Chinese, Japanese and Korean. In Western buildings, 13 is sometimes skipped too, which produces the long jumps in some towers.

How developers handle it

  • Skipping 4, 14, 24, 34, 44 and sometimes the whole 40s.
  • Renaming floors as 3A or 5A, or using letters.
  • Choosing unit and street numbers ending in 8 for marketing.

What it means for price

In markets with many Chinese buyers, agents widely report that 4-heavy addresses sell more slowly or need a discount, and addresses with 8 are marketed as a feature. Size varies by market; treat it as a factor in your negotiation, not a fixed rule.

If you're buying or selling

  • Buying: a 4 address can be a bargaining chip.
  • Selling: lead with other strengths; consider a feng shui staging note.
  • Developing: number units deliberately. It costs nothing and removes objections.
